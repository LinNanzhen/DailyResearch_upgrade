Periodontitis, a leading cause of tooth loss worldwide, arises from a dysregulated host immune response, and males consistently show higher prevalence and more severe disease than females. This disparity historically has been attributed to behavioral/environmental factors, but growing evidence indicates that biological sex, acting through hormonal signaling and sex chromosome-linked genetic mechanisms, independently shapes periodontal immune responses and subsequent bone resorption. In this review, we examine preclinical and clinical evidence on the impact of biological sex on host modulation therapies to prevent periodontitis. The IL-1β/inflammasome pathway and osteoclast differentiation via receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL)/osteoprotegerin (OPG) signaling both show clear sex-dependent regulation in experimental periodontitis, illustrating the biological complexity that single-target therapies must address in this heterogeneous condition.
