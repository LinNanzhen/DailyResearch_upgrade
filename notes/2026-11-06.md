Community water fluoridation (CWF) is an effective public health intervention to prevent dental caries. This study investigated potential associations between lifetime fluoride exposure from CWF and cognitive outcomes in older Irish adults and whether early-life exposure modified these associations. Data from the Irish Longitudinal Study on Ageing (TILDA; wave 3, 2014 to 2015) were analyzed (n = 6,684). Lifetime (≥50 y) and early-life (ages 0 to 5 y) fluoride exposures were estimated by linking residential histories to national water fluoride monitoring data and were available for N = 2,495 participants (mean age 65.32 y, standard deviation 8.64 y). Primary exposures were cumulative lifetime exposure to fluoridated water (CLEFW; continuous) and percentage lifetime exposure to fluoridated water (%LEFW), classified as low ( 75%). Cognitive outcomes were Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) and Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) error scores (30 minus raw scores), with higher scores indicating poorer cognition. Associations were examined using multivariable negative binomial regression models, adjusted for sociodemographic, lifestyle, health, loneliness, and health care access facto
