The combined effects of chronic maternal periodontitis on offspring gut microbiome and brain outcomes remain unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the effects of maternal oral microbiome disruption on brain function and gut microbiome in offspring. Periodontitis was induced in 11- to 12-wk-old parous C57BL/6J mice by maintaining ligatures around both sides of the maxillary second molars for 4 wk, followed by breeding with naïve male mice. Maternal oral and gut microbiome, as well as the offspring gut microbiome, was analyzed by 16S rRNA gene sequencing. Male offspring were evaluated at 7 to 8 wk of age via a set of analyses, including behavioral tests, microbiome profiling, RNA sequencing, immunohistochemistry for glial cell populations, and in vivo calcium imaging. Cerebellar DNA methylation was assessed at embryonic day 18.5 to examine early epigenetic alterations. Maternal periodontitis significantly reduced microbial diversity in the maternal oral microbiome and altered the composition of the maternal gut microbiome.
