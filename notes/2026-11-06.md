The global rise in metabolic diseases has increased the severity and therapeutic complexity of oral inflammatory disorders. Persistent inflammation driven by metabolic memory presents a central pathological mechanism. Even after metabolic parameters are normalized, abnormal gene expression can persist, leading to prolonged disease progression and recurrent episodes. In the oral cavity, metabolic memory is most clearly evident in periodontitis and impaired wound healing, where sustained immune dysregulation and tissue destruction remain after hyperglycemia or hyperlipidemia has been corrected. Epigenetic reprogramming presents a key molecular mechanism of metabolic memory, including DNA methylation, histone modifications, and expression of noncoding RNAs. The epigenetic alterations collectively reprogram immune cell function, driving the dysregulation of innate and adaptive immunity while influencing the fate of oral stromal cells and epithelial cells.
