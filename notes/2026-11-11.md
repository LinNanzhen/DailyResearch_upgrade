BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Adult orthodontic treatment is often prolonged due to biological factors. This randomized split-mouth clinical trial evaluated the effects of low-level laser therapy (LLLT) on orthodontic tooth movement in adults by assessing osteocalcin (OC) and N-telopeptide (NTx) levels as biomarkers of bone remodeling, together with the rate of canine distalization. MATERIALS AND METHODS: Thirty adults (18-35 years) requiring extraction of the maxillary first premolars and canine distalization were included. LLLT (980 nm, 1000 mW, 15 s/point) was applied to selected hemiarches immediately after appliance activation and every two weeks for four months. Gingival crevicular fluid samples were collected before, and 14 days after activation to determine OC and NTx concentrations by ELISA. Canine distalization was measured monthly over four months. RESULTS: Laser-treated hemiarches showed a significant increase in OC levels compared with baseline (mean difference 0.251 ng/mL, p < 0.0001), whereas changes in control hemiarches were not significant.
